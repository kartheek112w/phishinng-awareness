Pause. Inspect. Report.

Practical phishing awareness: recognize social engineering before it becomes an incident.

What is phishing?

Phishing is a form of social engineering in which an attacker impersonates a trusted person or organization to persuade someone to reveal information, open a harmful attachment, approve a request, or visit a deceptive site.

Look at the sender

Check the complete address, not just the display name. Watch for misspellings and unrelated domains.

Inspect the destination

Hover over links on desktop or use the link-preview feature. Navigate to known services using a saved bookmark.

Question urgency

Threats, secrecy, unexpected rewards, and pressure to act immediately are common manipulation tactics.

Fictional example: account warning

From: IT Helpdesk <support@it-helpdesk.example>

Subject: Immediate action required — mailbox closure

Your mailbox will be disabled in 30 minutes. Open the attached file and sign in to confirm your password.

Warning signs: unexpected threat, artificial deadline, password request, attachment, and sender domain that may not match the real organization. The reserved .example domain is used for training.

Common social-engineering tactics

  • Authority: pretending to be an executive, bank, or IT administrator.
  • Fear and urgency: claiming an account or payment will be blocked.
  • Curiosity: using unexpected documents, invoices, or shared files.
  • Reciprocity or reward: offering a prize or benefit in exchange for action.
  • Business email compromise: impersonating a colleague or supplier to change payment details.

Best practices

  1. Verify unusual requests using a separate, trusted channel.
  2. Do not enter passwords after following an unexpected email link.
  3. Use multifactor authentication and a password manager.
  4. Keep browsers, operating systems, and endpoint protection updated.
  5. Report suspicious messages; do not forward them broadly.
  6. If you clicked, report promptly, change affected credentials through the official site, and follow incident-response guidance.
